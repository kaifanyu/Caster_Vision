# Angular motion recovery under blur: alternatives to point tracking

Research checked on **2026-09-18**. Scope: two calibrated 30 Hz cameras observing a known caster with a shared roll and two independently spinning, separated hemispheres. This is a literature and implementation assessment; no model was installed, no weights were downloaded, and no existing pipeline was modified. Recommendations below are engineering inferences for this mechanism, distinguished from published results.

The most promising accuracy upgrade is **to fit the raw blurred images with the known mechanism and exposure integration**, using learned flow or restoration only to initialize difficult intervals. A sharper-looking video alone does not establish more accurate orientation or recover an unknown phase bridge. For a short first experiment, compare raw-image SEA-RAFT or WAFT proposals against the existing tracker before investing in restoration.

| Priority | Method family | Reuses the existing recordings? | Role in this caster problem | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exposure-integrated inverse rendering, inspired by Motion-from-Blur and MBA-VO | Yes | Optimize the three physical angle trajectories directly against both blurred image streams | Requires a trustworthy texture/reference map, exposure model, occlusion handling and good initialization |
| 2 | Dense optical flow: SEA-RAFT / WAFT, with RAFT as a reproducible older baseline | Yes | Supply distributed short-interval motion constraints when corner tracks are sparse | Flow can still be wrong under severe blur or occlusion; it cannot reconnect an unknown material phase by itself |
| 3 | RVRT video deblurring; NAFNet as a single-image control | Yes | Propose correspondences or initialize texture, then verify in the original images | Restoration is an inferred image, not an additional observation or a validated angular measurement |
| 4 | Image as an IMU: learned velocity from blur | Yes, experimentally | Secondary velocity proposal or a design reference for mechanism-specific training | Published formulation estimates camera motion; independently rotating shells violate that shared scene-motion model |
| 5 | Event-camera contrast maximization | Requires new event recordings | Obtain fine-time edge motion during fast rotation and fuse it with RGB reference/phase observations | Existing webcam AVI files contain no actual events; pure camera-rotation warps must be replaced by mechanism-specific warps |

**1. Exposure-integrated inverse rendering: strongest fit to the known geometry.** Motion-from-Blur (CVPR 2022) estimates object motion, shape and appearance by rendering intermediate states and averaging them over exposure intervals; it also estimates exposure gaps. Its target is moving objects, which makes its image-formation model more directly relevant here than ordinary camera-pose estimation. The authors provide an implementation. [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Rozumnyi_Motion-From-Blur_3D_Shape_and_Motion_Estimation_of_Motion-Blurred_Objects_in_CVPR_2022_paper.html), [official code](https://github.com/rozumden/MotionFromBlur).

For this project, my recommendation is to adapt that principle while fixing the already measured hemisphere geometry, camera intrinsics/extrinsics and mounted pivot. Optimize only shared roll, red/green phase curves and justified photometric/timing nuisance parameters. Use a sharp, observed surface texture atlas where available. The optimizer should re-create the observed blur, including the gap and shell visibility, rather than compare an instantaneous sharp render with a blurred frame. This is a proposed adaptation, not a claim that the published repository already supports this caster.

MBA-VO (ICCV 2021) is a useful second implementation reference: it estimates the camera trajectory during an exposure and re-blurs a sharp reference for direct alignment. It is a camera-odometry system, so its existing motion model should not be applied wholesale to the two shells. Its relevant contribution is explicit exposure-time image formation and direct residual evaluation. [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Liu_MBA-VO_Motion_Blur_Aware_Visual_Odometry_ICCV_2021_paper.html), [official code](https://github.com/ethliup/MBA-VO).

Inferred implementation order: keep geometry fixed, start with per-camera gain/offset and measured exposure durations, render several temporal samples per exposure, then increase sampling until estimates are stable. Treat rolling-shutter readout as a separately checked model; a fixed inter-camera offset and exposure integration do not automatically explain row-dependent capture time. Do not add a high-dimensional learned scene representation merely to fit this known three-angle mechanism.

**2026 follow-up checked: Recovering 3D Shapes from Ultra-Fast Motion-Blurred Images (3DV 2026).** The contribution is a faster differentiable blur renderer based on reusing barycentric-coordinate calculations, with a reported speedup up to 4.57 times. Its main optimization assumes known camera poses and motion parameters and recovers shape; the paper explicitly discusses that dependency and linear-motion/photometric assumptions. This is relevant as a possible renderer acceleration, not an already demonstrated solver for this caster's unknown angular trajectory. [Paper and acceptance information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07860), [full paper, especially Section 7 and Appendix M](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07860v1).

On 2026-09-18 the project page still labels code as coming soon, and the linked repository contains only an under-construction README. It is therefore not a runnable replacement to adopt now. My recommendation is to retain fixed hemisphere geometry and the existing motion parameterization; reconsider this renderer only if exposure integration becomes a measured computational bottleneck and implementation code becomes available. [Project](https://maxmilite.github.io/rec-from-ultrafast-blur/), [linked code repository](https://github.com/Maxmilite/rec-from-ultrafast-blur).

**2. Dense flow constrained by the mechanism: the cheapest useful comparison.** RAFT estimates dense inter-frame optical flow using all-pairs correlations and recurrent updates. SEA-RAFT (ECCV 2024) adds a mixture-of-Laplace training objective and publishes a custom example that outputs flow and uncertainty. Those make SEA-RAFT a useful candidate for selecting distributed, confident surface observations instead of depending only on sparse corners. [RAFT paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123470392.pdf), [official RAFT code](https://github.com/princeton-vl/RAFT), [official SEA-RAFT code and model instructions](https://github.com/princeton-vl/SEA-RAFT).

As of this review, the same lab's WAFT repository identifies the method as ICLR 2026 and provides a newer optical-flow implementation based on high-resolution warping rather than a cost volume. Include it as a current comparison, without assuming its general benchmark results establish better blurred-shell tracking. Pin the chosen repository revision and checkpoint in the experiment record; do not compare unspecified moving defaults. [Official WAFT code](https://github.com/princeton-vl/WAFT).

My proposed use is to fit the predicted image displacement induced by the three mechanism angles to dense flow in each camera, at that camera's native timestamps. Reject yoke/background pixels, silhouette/occlusion boundaries, inconsistent forward/backward flow and low-confidence observations. Then verify candidate motion using raw pixels and both cameras. Large point counts are correlated evidence, not thousands of independent angular measurements. These networks remain two-image correspondence estimators; a coherent-looking flow field is not proof of preserved identity through a long tracking gap.

**3. RVRT deblurring: a controlled preprocessing experiment.** RVRT (NeurIPS 2022) uses temporal feature aggregation and guided deformable attention for video restoration; its official implementation includes video-deblurring models trained on GoPro or DVD. Its published task is restoration, not caster-angle recovery. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146), [official code, datasets and checkpoints](https://github.com/JingyunLiang/RVRT).

NAFNet (ECCV 2022) provides image-deblurring models, including a setting involving JPEG artifacts. It is a useful single-frame control against RVRT's temporal aggregation. Its separate NAFSSR stereo-super-resolution model should not be mistaken for calibrated two-camera deblurring or motion recovery. [Official NAFNet code and task list](https://github.com/megvii-research/NAFNet).

My recommendation is an ablation: raw frames versus RVRT-restored frames versus NAFNet-restored frames, followed by the same correspondence and metric fitting stages. Preserve the original image index and timestamp. Do not count invented intermediate frames as measurements. Temporal restoration may yield steadier features, but synthesized edges, displaced markings or phase-averaged texture could bias motion. This is an inverse-problem risk, not a claim that these particular models always hallucinate. Accept improvements only when orientation/timing errors improve against independent evidence; visual sharpness and restoration scores alone do not answer that question.

**4. Image as an IMU: interesting blur information, substantial model mismatch.** This ICCV 2025 method predicts dense motion flow and depth from a blurred image, then estimates instantaneous camera velocity under a small-motion approximation. Its official example requires focal length and exposure time and explicitly notes velocity-sign ambiguity from a single image. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17358), [official code and example](https://github.com/jerredchen/image-as-an-imu).

For this caster, the two shells have different motion and the yoke/background can have another motion. My inference is that the released camera-motion output is unsuitable as a direct shell-angle measurement. A narrower research experiment could replace the camera-motion fit with the known three-angle image-motion Jacobian, or train on mechanism-specific synthetic blur, using adjacent frames to disambiguate sign. Rank it below direct geometry and dense flow until this transfer is validated. A physically impossible sign or a velocity that only explains the background must not silently become the common caster state.

**5. Event cameras: a future acquisition option, not recovery from existing AVI.** The angular-velocity work of Gallego and Scaramuzza and the CVPR 2018 contrast-maximization framework estimate motion by aligning timestamped brightness-change events. Their rotational examples concern camera rotation. [Original angular-velocity paper](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/RAL16_Gallego.pdf), [contrast-maximization paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Gallego_A_Unifying_Contrast_CVPR_2018_paper.html).

For an available author implementation that also addresses accumulated orientation drift, Kim et al.'s globally aligned events method combines local-window alignment with previously aligned events. The author project provides code and datasets with VICON ground truth. [Author project](https://haram-kim.github.io/Globally_Aligned_Events/), [official code](https://github.com/Haram-kim/Globally_Aligned_Events).

For future fast-spin experiments, my recommendation is a calibrated event camera observing textured shell regions together with RGB reference frames. Warp events using the physical shell surface and three-angle model, mask the stationary yoke, and keep event/RGB clocks aligned. An RGB-to-event simulation cannot recover genuinely unrecorded timing from a 30 Hz AVI. Events also do not automatically resolve shell symmetry, missing revolutions or pose drift: retain texture anchors or an independent angular reference. Whether this hardware is worth adopting should be determined after the raw-video experiments show the remaining error is acquisition-limited.

**Temporal accuracy, ambiguity and acceptance criteria.** A blurred exposure aggregates multiple physical instants. More than one motion history can explain similar image evidence; sharper reconstructed samples do not remove that ambiguity. The authors of Animation from Blur explicitly model multiple plausible solutions and require motion guidance to choose among them. This supports treating recovered sub-frame sequences as hypotheses rather than new observations. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10123), [official code](https://github.com/zzh-tech/Animation-from-Blur).

For this project, I recommend these experiment gates:

1. Evaluate at original corrected camera times and explicitly defined exposure-center times. Measure inter-camera delay and phase lag as well as angle error.
2. Compare on sharp held-out views or an independently measured reference, plus synthetic clips with known angular trajectories, differing camera exposures, occlusion and noise. The earlier estimated trajectory is an initialization or comparator, not ground truth.
3. Record wrapped orientation error, unwrapped phase/turn errors, angular-velocity error, coverage, false confident updates and failure recovery. A smooth curve with the wrong number of turns is a failure.
4. Check candidate trajectories against the untouched blurred frames. For inverse rendering, check exposure-integrated residuals; for restored-image proposals, require independent raw-image/stereo consistency.
5. Separate local angular change from accumulated phase observability. If tracking resumes after an unobserved phase bridge, keep the phase as prior-dependent until real anchor evidence reconnects it. No restoration or flow model should promote that state merely because its outputs look coherent.

The practical hierarchy is therefore: **raw dense-flow comparison first for cost, mechanism-specific exposure integration first for the main accuracy investment, restoration only as a tested proposal branch, learned blur-to-velocity as a secondary research branch, and event sensing for future acquisition if the current exposures have destroyed the necessary evidence**. None of the reviewed papers establishes that its reported benchmark gain transfers automatically to this two-hemisphere setup.
